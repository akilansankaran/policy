Bridging Gaps in Care: Strategic Policy Initiatives for Enhancing Health Care Services for Aging Women

Health Policy Research Team, Harvard IOP Policy Program
Spring 2024
Policy Brief
✓ Open Access
10.xxxx/IOP.HEALTH.2024.001 (registration pending)

Abstract

Women over the age of 65 represent one of the fastest-growing segments of the U.S. population, yet federal healthcare policy has historically treated aging and gender as separate analytic categories. This brief identifies structural gaps in Medicare coverage, community-based care availability, and preventive service delivery that disproportionately affect aging women — particularly those in rural areas and those from racial and ethnic minority communities.

Drawing on data from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, the Administration for Community Living, and peer-reviewed public health literature, we evaluate the adequacy of current benefit structures and propose a three-part policy framework: (1) expanding Medicare coverage for gender-specific preventive services, (2) strengthening the long-term care workforce through federal wage and training incentives, and (3) scaling community health navigator programs in medically underserved areas. Our recommendations are designed for uptake by Senate Finance Committee staff, state Medicaid directors, and nonprofit healthcare commissioners.

Background

By 2030, one in five Americans will be over 65. Women constitute 56% of this population and are significantly more likely than men to live alone, live in poverty, and require long-term care services. Despite these demographic realities, Medicare's benefit structure — largely unchanged since its 1965 design — does not adequately account for conditions that disproportionately affect women, including osteoporosis, Alzheimer's disease, and pelvic floor disorders.

At the same time, the long-term care workforce — which is itself overwhelmingly female and disproportionately composed of workers of color — faces severe staffing shortages, low wages, and high turnover rates. The intersection of patient and provider vulnerability creates a compounding care crisis that existing policy frameworks are ill-equipped to address.

Policy Recommendations

1. Expand Medicare Preventive Service Coverage. We recommend Congressional action to include routine bone density screenings, pelvic floor therapy, and cognitive health assessments within Medicare Part B's annual wellness visit framework, removing cost-sharing requirements that currently suppress utilization among low-income beneficiaries.

2. Strengthen the Long-Term Care Workforce. We propose a federal matching program incentivizing states to raise minimum wages for home health aides to $20/hour, paired with a federally-funded training and credentialing pathway modeled on existing apprenticeship frameworks.

3. Scale Community Health Navigator Programs. We recommend expanding the ACA's Community Health Worker grant program with a dedicated funding stream for organizations serving aging women in rural and tribal areas, with program design informed by successful models in New Mexico and rural Appalachia.
